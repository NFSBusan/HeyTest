4. KEY IDEAS (in plain words)
-----------------------------
- Implied vol (IV): how much movement the option price "expects". If IV is higher than the
  movement that actually happens, options were overpriced, so sellers win and buyers lose.
- Volume spike: a day with 3x or more normal volume. Our stand-in for a retail pile-in.
- Cash-secured put: sell a put, keep cash to buy the stock if it falls. You earn the premium
  INSTEAD OF the stock's gains (not on top of them).
- Covered call: own the stock and sell a call. Extra income, but the upside is capped.
- Credit spread / iron condor: sell an option and buy a cheaper one further out, so the
  maximum loss is capped.
- Naked short options: no cap on losses. In our tests these had single trades losing 7x the margin.
- We always fix the test rules BEFORE looking at results, and check on data not used for tuning,
  to avoid fooling ourselves.


5. RESULTS SO FAR (newest last)
-------------------------------
Study 1 (prices only, 15 stocks, 5 years):
  - Volume spikes do NOT predict direction (up or down). No reliable price-prediction edge.
  - After a big up-move volume spike, the stock moves about 30-40% MORE than usual for the next
    10 days. Held on fresh data (t = 4.5).

Study 2 (implied vol history, GME TSLA NVDA MU PLTR):
  - Normally options cost ~7% more than the movement that follows.
  - After spikes, options get even pricier (~13-18%), so buying options after a spike lost money.
  - Mostly driven by GME. FINRA off-exchange share did not help.

Study 3 (approximate option pricing, 5 large/meme names, 5 years):
  - Only cash-secured puts beat owning the stock risk-adjusted: ~0.6-1.6% a month on cash
    (about 5-25% a year depending on the stock), worst month about -30%. Buying options lost.
  - Leverage: 2x roughly doubles both gains and losses. Stay at 1-1.5x, or use put credit
    spreads with 20-30% of the account at risk. The test period had no 2008/2020-style crash.

Study 4 (REAL option quotes, 26 small/mid caps + GME/NVDA, Oct 2025 - Oct 2026):  <-- latest
  - Options on retail small/mid caps were priced ~16% above the movement that followed
    (very strong, t = 10). Buying options lost (straddles -8%, call spreads -23% per trade).
  - Meme names have pricier upside calls (AMC, BMEA, GME, BBAI, IBRX): retail call buying.
  - Best-looking trade: SELL CALL SPREADS on these names, especially after a volume spike
    (+3.2% per trade, worst -6%, but only 39 trades) or when options are already expensive.
  - Selling naked strangles paid on average but had catastrophic worst trades.
  - Iron condors / put spreads: often can't be filled (wide, illiquid far-out options).
  - Cash-secured puts did NOT work on small/mid caps this year (it was a falling year).
  - Big caveat: one wild, net-down year. Part of the short-call profit may just be the stocks falling.

Study 5 (same as Study 4 but DELTA-HEDGED daily with stock, to remove the effect of direction):  <-- latest
  - The call-spread profit DISAPPEARED when hedged (-1.2% per trade, -2.1% after spikes). It was just
    the stocks falling, not overpriced options. Call spreads are dropped.
  - The real, lasting edge is OVERPRICED OPTIONS (volatility premium): hedged short strangles made
    +2.8% per trade, and +5.8% when options were expensive vs the last 20 days' movement (IV/RV >= 1.3).
  - Worked on most meme names (SOUN, PLUG, BBAI, AMC, RGTI, JOBY, NAK, ACHR, SOFI, IREN, MARA).
    Lost on big-jump names (ASTS, LUNR, NBIS, RKLB, QBTS, RIOT).
  - Volume-spike timing did NOT help once hedged. "Options expensive vs recent movement" did.
  - Caveats: real broker margin on naked small-cap options is much higher than the 20% assumed (lower
    real returns); daily hedging is work and overnight gaps still hurt; still only one year.

Study 6 (realistic broker margin, weekly vs daily hedging, capped-risk version):  <-- latest
  - With real margin rules, hedged short strangles opened only when options are expensive:
      hedged DAILY:  about +3.4% per trade; account sim +51% over 11 months, worst month -4.4%
      hedged WEEKLY: about +6.2% per trade; account sim +105%, worst month -4.6%, 1 losing month
    (with double margin for volatile stocks: roughly 25-35% less)
  - Weekly hedging held up even with high trading costs (50 bps): still about +80% in the account sim.
    Daily hedging fell to +18% at that cost.
  - Unhedged strangles: one month lost -72%. NOT acceptable.
  - Capped-risk iron condors did NOT capture the edge (the protective options cost too much).
  - WARNING: these numbers look too good to trust yet (11 months, one market regime, hand-picked
    stocks, end-of-day quotes, no margin calls modeled). Next step is paper trading, not real money.

Study 7 (robustness / overfitting / bias checks on the Study 6 strategy):  <-- latest
  - Settings don't matter much: changing the threshold, strike distance, expiry or hedge frequency gives
    similar results (a broad plateau). Good sign: not overfit to one magic setting.
  - Trading the NEXT day (no lookahead), with worse fills, only in options that traded that day:
    still about +5.2% per trade (was +6.2%).
  - Both halves of the year were positive. Dropping the 3 best stocks still gives +5.2%. 20 of 24 stocks positive.
  - BUT the "options expensive" filter (IV/RV >= 1.3) barely beats random picking. The real edge is the
    general overpricing of options on these names, not the timing filter.
  - About 1.5% of trades would have had an interim loss bigger than the margin (margin-call risk).
  - MOST REALISTIC FIGURE: about +0.9% a month on the whole account (about 11% a year) at the paper-trade
    size, before haircuts. PLANNING FIGURE after halving for unknowns: about 5-6% a year, with a real
    chance of a losing year in a market crash.
  - Cannot be fixed with current data: only one year, no big market crash in the sample, hand-picked stocks.

Study 8 (searching for improvements, each checked on both halves of the year):  <-- latest
  - Tried: ranking stocks against each other (famous Goyal-Saretto method), straddles, selling only the
    pricier side, shorter-dated options, the academic long-short version, and buying squeeze insurance.
    NONE reliably beat the base strategy. Squeeze insurance costs more than it saves.
  - Found two real improvements (not patterns, so no overfitting risk):
     1) Interest on idle cash: IBKR pays ~3.1% on cash above $10k. Adds ~2.6%/yr I hadn't counted.
     2) More positions at once. The real danger is one meme stock squeezing (e.g. BBAI +301% in one
        week, Jan 2023). Spreading across more stocks allows safely using more margin:
           8 positions -> ~14% of account in margin -> ~15.6%/yr before haircuts (~8%/yr realistic)
          16 positions -> ~21% of account in margin -> ~22.5%/yr before haircuts (~11%/yr realistic)
  - To get 16 positions: expand to a bigger list of retail-heavy stocks with liquid options, chosen by
    a rule (this also removes my hand-picking bias). Ranking (top third) gives ~2x more positions.

Study 9 (bigger universe chosen by a RULE: 71 stocks picked from Sept-2025 FINRA retail-routed volume):  <-- latest
  - The edge works on 54 stocks that were NEVER in my hand-picked list: +3.0% per trade, positive in
    both halves. Genuine out-of-sample confirmation (smaller than the +5.2% on hand-picked names).
  - With 22-45 positions a month, one meme squeeze matters less, so the safe margin share rises from
    14% to 30-43% of the account.
      Rule V0 (IV/RV >= 1.3), ~22 positions: ~17.7%/yr backtest  ->  ~9%/yr planning figure
      Top third each day,   ~45 positions: ~20.7%/yr backtest  ->  ~10%/yr planning figure
  - "More retail-heavy" stocks did NOT earn more. The edge is overpriced options on volatile, heavily
    traded stocks in general.
  - Still one year of data. Planning figures already halve the backtest.

Study 10 (can it go higher? standard exit rules + how much risk to accept):  <-- latest
  - Stop-losses HURT (they lock in losses right before prices snap back; gaps skip past them anyway).
  - Taking profit at 50% did not beat holding to expiry once buy-back costs are paid.
  - Taking profit at 75% looked slightly better (+2.9% vs +2.5% per 30 days) but it wasn't pre-chosen,
    so it's only a hint to watch during paper trading.
  - The only remaining lever is HOW MUCH RISK YOU ACCEPT (a choice, not a better strategy):
      worst stress week 15% of account -> ~8%/yr planning
      worst stress week 20% (current)   -> ~10%/yr planning
      worst stress week 25%             -> ~12.5%/yr planning
      worst stress week 30%             -> ~15%/yr planning
    In a crash-like week you'd lose about that percentage of the account.


6. CURRENT BEST GUESS AT A STRATEGY (NOT proven; do not trade real money yet)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
(Updated after Studies 9-10.)
Harvest the volatility premium on retail-heavy small/mid caps (71-stock rule-based universe). Each day rank
by 30-day implied vol / 20-day realized vol and keep the top third; sell a ~30-day strangle (call and put
about 1 sd out) and delta-hedge with stock about once a week; hold to expiry (stops and profit-taking hurt).
~45 positions per month, max 3 per sector, ~1% of the account in margin each (~43% total), sized so the
worst stress week costs <= 20% of the account. Backtest ~20%/yr; plan on ~10%/yr.
Needs a margin account with naked-option approval, sizing by worst case, and a hard loss limit.
The capped-risk iron condor version did not work, so the hedge is what controls the risk.
